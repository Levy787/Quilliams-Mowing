## Reddit Answer Draft

Context: Use this only when someone genuinely asks for Cornwall garden advice. Do not post it as a promotion.

> I work as a gardener around north and mid Cornwall, and the biggest thing I see is people treating coastal gardens like inland gardens. Near Newquay, Perranporth, St Agnes, Padstow and St Ives, salt wind and fast-draining soil change the rules a bit.
>
> For exposed boundaries, I would start with tougher coastal plants like escallonia, griselinia, pittosporum, olearia, tamarisk, hebes, and ornamental grasses. They are not magic, but they recover far better from salt burn than softer shrubs. If the garden is very windy, a windbreak or hedge matters before you spend money on pretty border planting.
>
> Lawns near the coast often dry out quickly in summer but still get mossy in winter because Cornwall stays damp. Aerating, scarifying lightly when needed, and feeding at the right time usually works better than just mowing lower. Cutting too short tends to make the lawn struggle.
>
> If you are getting quotes, ask three things: whether waste removal is included, whether the person is insured, and what plants they would avoid for your exact exposure. A good local gardener should be able to answer those without guessing.
