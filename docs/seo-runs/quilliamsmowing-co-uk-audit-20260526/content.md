## 2. E-E-A-T assessment

### Experience (88/100) — strong

First-hand signals are clearly present and they read as real, not generated:

- About page tells a specific, falsifiable personal story (Tasmania farm, economics degree, accounting/sales career, five years of "what if", moved to Cornwall 2025, started Quilliams). Specific = credible.
- Area pages reference real neighbourhoods (Malpas, Kenwyn, Tregolls, Langarth in Truro; Pentire, Fistral, Cranstock Street, Trenance Valley in Newquay; Mithian, Mingoose, Peterville, Trevaunance Cove, Wheal Coates in St Agnes). These are not just town names swapped — they show someone who has actually been there.
- Project case studies describe what was done, in what order, with material call-outs (heavy-duty weed membrane, rustic edging, lily bed mulched). This is the kind of specificity that distinguishes a real practitioner from a templated content factory.
- Named customer reviews with neighbourhood attribution on area pages (Matthew Wellington in Truro, James Perrin in Newquay, Robert Jakes mentioned in Newquay narrative). Pseudonyms aside, the granular attribution helps.

What is missing:

- No process photos on most service pages (only the projects pages carry photos). Adding 2-3 in-progress shots per service page would be a strong Experience signal.
- No dated "last job completed in [area]" or "this week I worked on..." freshness signals.

### Expertise (82/100) — good

- Technical specificity is consistent: clay vs sandy soils named by area, salt-tolerant plant species recommended (griselinia, escallonia, pittosporum, olearia, hebe), correct horticultural reasoning (cutting blade higher in summer to cope with dry spells, scarifying/aerating for compacted clay around Treliske and Langarth, why pampas root ball removal takes time).
- Pricing ranges are concrete (£20 small lawn, £5-15 per metre hedge, £60-120 maintenance visit, £1,500-2,500 gravel garden). Concrete numbers signal expertise.
- About page lists credentials: public liability insured, licensed waste carrier, 120+ projects, 5.0 Google.

What is missing:

- No author byline on service or area pages. Only the blog post carries a "By Levi Quilliam" line with a date. Service and area pages should also expose the author for E-E-A-T crawlers and AI citation systems.
- No "qualifications" or "training" page or any explicit horticultural certification (RHS, City & Guilds, etc.). If Levi has none, that is fine, but the "farm in Tasmania + 120 projects" framing should be made more prominent on every service page header (currently only on About).
- No outbound citations to authoritative sources (RHS, Cornwall Council waste-carrier register, Met Office climate data). One or two contextual outbound links would lift the page-level expertise signal.

### Authoritativeness (70/100) — weakest dimension

- AggregateRating schema is present on the homepage. Good.
- Named reviews on area pages. Good.
- One competitor-comparison blog post citing other Newquay operators with nofollow outbound links. Good.

What is missing:

- No press, citations, or local-paper mentions. (Pitch Cornwall Live / West Briton / Newquay Voice with a local-interest angle — "Tasmanian farmer turned Cornish gardener" is a press-friendly story.)
- No guest posts, no inclusion in third-party "best of" lists, no industry directory presence visible from the site.
- Google Business Profile is referenced ("5.0 on Google") but the site does not link out to it for verification. Add a direct GBP link in the footer.
- No social proof beyond on-site reviews. If there are Trustpilot / Checkatrade / Yell reviews, surface them.

### Trustworthiness (87/100) — strong

- NAP (name, address, phone) is consistent across the footer of every page checked.
- Public liability and waste carrier credentials repeated everywhere. Strong trust signal for a trade.
- Privacy policy and terms linked from every footer.
- HTTPS, clear contact methods, named owner with photo and biography.
- Quote process is transparent (photos in, fixed price out, no hidden extras language).
- FAQs are specific and answer real objections (e.g. "Do you only do ongoing maintenance?", "Can I get a quote without a site visit?").

What is missing:

- No explicit "money-back" or "satisfaction" guarantee language. Even a one-line "if you are not happy with the finish I will come back and put it right" would add Trust.
- Waste carrier licence number is not displayed. Putting the actual registration number in the footer (Environment Agency CBDU-style ID) would be a meaningful Trust upgrade.
- Insurer name and policy number reference (just the insurer, not the policy) would also help. "Public liability cover with [Insurer]" is more trustworthy than "fully insured".

## 6. AI citation readiness (84/100)

Modern AI search (Gemini, Perplexity, ChatGPT, Copilot) cites pages that have crisp, lift-out answer formats. This site does this well in places and poorly in others.

**Strong:**

- FAQPage schema is present on the homepage, blog post, and pricing page with Question/Answer pairs. Strong AI lift signal.
- Pricing is given in concrete numbers ("£20 for a small lawn", "£5-15 per metre", "£60-120 per visit"). These are exactly the kind of factoids AI engines extract.
- "Quick answer" box at the top of the blog post is exemplary — that pattern (TL;DR upfront, evidence below) should be replicated on every service page.
- Area pages include "Garden Challenges in [town]" sections that are extractable, factual, and locally distinctive. These are AI-citation gold.

**Weaknesses:**

- Service pages do not open with a "Quick answer" / definition block. Add a 2-3 sentence lift box at the top of every service page (e.g. "Lawn care in Cornwall typically means a fortnightly mow with edging and strimming, costing £25-50 per visit for an average garden. Here is how I structure it.").
- FAQ schemas exist but the FAQ answers visible on the rendered page are often collapsed behind UI. Make sure the answer text is in the DOM at load time (i.e. server-rendered) — partially the case already.
- No "How to" structured snippets. /pricing could carry a HowTo schema ("How to estimate gardening costs in Cornwall").
- No definition-list (`<dl>`) markup detected. Definition lists are AI-friendly for terminology (e.g. "What is a green waste carrier licence?"). Use them on About, Pricing, and the landscaping service page.

User preferences (per memory): first-person, no em dashes.

**Em-dash audit:**

| Page | Em-dashes found |
|---|---|
| / (homepage) | **26** (high — pre-remediation copy) |
| /pricing | **6** (medium) |
| /about | 2 (likely from a quote or layout chrome) |
| All other audited pages | 2 (chrome only — header/footer rendering) |

The 2 em-dashes appearing on every page are almost certainly from a shared header/footer or service-card component. The 26 on the homepage and 6 on /pricing are content em-dashes that need replacement.

**Homepage em-dash contexts found (replace these):**

- "outdoor spaces—without the physical work or time commitment" → "outdoor spaces, without the physical work or time commitment"
- "easy to maintain—season after season" → "easy to maintain, season after season"
- "back under control — then leave it tidy and manageable" → "back under control, then leave it tidy and manageable"
- "reach out — we'll confirm availability quickly" → "reach out and we'll confirm availability quickly"
- "back under control—clean lines, shaped growth..." → "back under control, with clean lines..." (appears twice — in a duplicated project card)
- "tidy, and manageable—done cleanly with a sharp finish" → "tidy, and manageable, done cleanly with a sharp finish" (twice)
- Review: "super friendly and reliable — highly recommend!" — this is inside a customer testimonial quote, leave it as the customer wrote it.

**Pricing em-dashes:** Found in the pricing-guide intro and "Estimates are a guide only — every yard is different". Replace with commas, semicolons, or full stops.

**HIGH severity:** Homepage em-dashes violate house style on the most-trafficked page. Fix this immediately.

**Tone tightening notes for homepage:**

- The "Quality / Satisfaction / Sustainability" three-card block reads like generic agency boilerplate. Rewrite each card in Levi's voice (e.g. "I do the job right — sharp lines, proper edges, every time" becomes "Sharp lines, proper edges, every time. I would rather work slower than leave it looking rushed.").
- "we help homeowners enjoy clean, healthy, and beautiful outdoor spaces" — switch to first person to match the rest of the site, or remove. The site uses "I" everywhere else; the homepage's "we" is jarring and undermines the personal brand built on /about.

## 9. Blog depth assessment

One published post. The post itself is well-built (1,350 words, listicle format with named competitors, Quick Answer schema, comparison table, internal links back to /areas/newquay and /services). It is a solid SEO play but it cannot carry the topical authority load on its own.

**The single-post problem:**

- Google's topical authority models reward depth across a cluster. One post = no cluster.
- The blog index page is functionally empty (one entry), which weakens internal linking and AI-engine confidence that this site is an authoritative information source.
- The post's date is "Updated 2026-05-04" — fresh, but solo. Without companion posts the freshness signal is isolated.

Target: 6 published blog posts within 90 days, then 1-2 new posts per month thereafter. Topics below.

## 10. Top 5 blog post topics to write next

Ranked by combination of search demand, intent match to Levi's services, ease of writing from genuine experience, and AI-citation likelihood.

### 1. Best plants for coastal Cornwall gardens (salt and wind tolerant)

- **Target keyword:** `best plants for coastal Cornwall gardens`
- **Secondary:** `salt tolerant plants Cornwall`, `wind tolerant hedge plants Cornwall`, `coastal garden plants UK`
- **Intent:** Informational, top-of-funnel. Homeowners researching what they can actually plant in exposed gardens. Strong AI-citation potential.
- **Why now:** Three area pages (Newquay, St Agnes, plus likely Perranporth and Padstow) already touch on salt-tolerant species. A pillar post consolidates topical authority and gives each area page somewhere to link to.
- **Suggested length:** 2,000-2,500 words.
- **Structure:** Quick answer table (plant / max wind tolerance / salt tolerance / ideal use), then 8-12 species with photo, mature size, where Levi has used them in Cornwall, and what he would warn against. Include griselinia, escallonia, pittosporum, olearia, hebe, tamarisk, sea buckthorn, euonymus, viburnum tinus.
- **Internal links:** /areas/newquay, /areas/st-agnes, /areas/perranporth, /services/landscaping.

### 2. How much does a gardener cost in Cornwall in 2026?

- **Target keyword:** `how much does a gardener cost UK`
- **Secondary:** `gardener prices Cornwall`, `gardener hourly rate UK 2026`, `lawn mowing prices Cornwall`
- **Intent:** Commercial investigation. Highest-converting blog intent. Pricing transparency is rare in the trade, and a clear post will win comparison searches.
- **Why now:** /pricing is thin (HIGH severity finding above). A long-form blog post covers the "how much" research query and lets /pricing focus on the calculator/quote conversion.
- **Suggested length:** 1,800-2,200 words.
- **Structure:** Quick answer (typical Cornwall range: £25-50 per hour, £60-120 per maintenance visit, £1,500-2,500 for a small landscaping project). Then breakdown by job type, regional comparison (Newquay vs Truro vs further out), why some gardeners are cheaper (and what to watch for), how to get an accurate quote.
- **Internal links:** /pricing, /quote, all six service pages.

### 3. When to scarify, aerate, and feed your lawn in Cornwall — month-by-month calendar

- **Target keyword:** `when to scarify lawn UK`
- **Secondary:** `lawn care calendar Cornwall`, `when to feed lawn UK`, `Cornwall lawn moss treatment`
- **Intent:** Informational, seasonal. Picks up evergreen search traffic and primes Levi as the local lawn-care expert.
- **Why now:** /services/lawn-care references scarifying, aerating, and seasonal feeding but no standalone resource exists. AI assistants love month-by-month calendars (highly extractable).
- **Suggested length:** 1,800-2,200 words.
- **Structure:** Quick answer ("scarify in March or September, aerate in autumn, feed in April and August"), then a month-by-month table for Jan-Dec specific to Cornwall's climate (mild winters mean different timing than the rest of the UK). Include moss-treatment notes for the damp inland clay around Truro vs the sandy coast.
- **Internal links:** /services/lawn-care, /areas/truro, /services/seasonal-cleanup.

### 4. How to remove an established pampas grass (and what to plant instead)

- **Target keyword:** `how to remove pampas grass`
- **Secondary:** `pampas grass root removal`, `pampas grass replacement plants`, `low maintenance pampas alternative`
- **Intent:** Informational with strong commercial pivot. People searching this are either DIY-ing it (free traffic, brand awareness) or about to give up and hire someone (direct lead).
- **Why now:** Levi already has a project page (`/projects/gravel-garden-with-patio`) that ends with a removed pampas grass. Repackaging that experience as a how-to gives the project a feeder post and demonstrates real practitioner expertise.
- **Suggested length:** 1,500-1,800 words.
- **Structure:** Quick answer (cut foliage low, dig root ball, expect a half-day with a mattock and a strong back), tool list, step-by-step with timing, what to plant or build in its place (link to gravel garden, low-maintenance shrubs from post #1), photos from Levi's actual project.
- **Internal links:** /projects/gravel-garden-with-patio, /services/landscaping, /services/garden-maintenance.

### 5. Hedge trimming in Cornwall — when, how often, and how much it costs

- **Target keyword:** `hedge trimming Cornwall cost`
- **Secondary:** `when to trim hedges UK`, `leylandii hedge maintenance`, `hedge cutting prices UK`
- **Intent:** Commercial investigation. Hedge work is a high-ticket service line and competitor coverage on this topic is weak.
- **Why now:** /services/hedge-trimming exists (per sitemap) and /projects/leylandii-hedge-trim-4m-tall provides case-study evidence. A pillar blog post anchors them both and captures the "how much" / "when" research queries.
- **Suggested length:** 1,800-2,200 words.
- **Structure:** Quick answer (most domestic hedges in Cornwall want trimming twice a year, May-June and August-September, at £5-15 per linear metre). Then sections on bird-nesting season and the law, species-specific timing (leylandii, beech, hornbeam, escallonia), reduction vs maintenance trims, when to call a tree surgeon instead, and what affects price.
- **Internal links:** /services/hedge-trimming, /projects/leylandii-hedge-trim-4m-tall, /pricing.
